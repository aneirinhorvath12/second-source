Dear Marcus,

I write in response to your quotation BRK/SCS/Q/022 for the additional pipe supports and vibration restraint on the suction header and the associated change to the support steelwork.

We have reviewed the quotation against your design submissions and the Employer's Requirements. The Requirements set performance criteria for the station: allowable vibration levels on the compressor suction and discharge pipework, and a maximum nozzle load at each compressor connection. They do not prescribe the number, type or location of supports. Those are matters for the Contractor's design.

As we read your covering narrative, the additional restraints follow from the detailed pipe stress and vibration analysis carried out after the Issue for Construction layouts were fixed. That analysis found that the support arrangement shown in your earlier design did not hold the header within the specified vibration limit at the first acoustic resonance. You have therefore added restraints and stiffened the supporting steelwork. This is the normal progression from a concept layout to a verified design. The criteria the design had to meet were the same before and after the analysis, and nothing in the Employer's Requirements has altered in the interval. We have found no Employer instruction or revised input in the quotation or its attachments that would account for the change.

On that basis, the work arises from developing Brackmoor's own design so that it meets the Employer's Requirements. Under clause 2.3 it forms part of the Contractor's obligations under the Contract, and no adjustment to the Contract Price is due in respect of it.

Please proceed on that basis. Your design should continue through the agreed review process, and the revised support drawings and the updated stress report should be submitted in the usual way so that our engineers can complete their comments. Craig Quigley will pick up any technical queries on the report. Megan Rowe will expect to see the revised support details before any steelwork is fabricated for the affected area.
