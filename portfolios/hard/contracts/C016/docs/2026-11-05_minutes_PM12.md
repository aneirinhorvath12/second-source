## 2. Health, safety and environment

60,000 hours worked in the period; no RIDDOR reportable incidents; 5 near misses reported.

Segregated walkways and vehicle routes have been reviewed around the new tanker bay. Delivery drivers were seen reversing without a banksman on two occasions last week. A second banksman will be rostered during peak delivery hours, and yellow lines have been repainted at the turning circle. Kieran Moffat has been notified of the change to the logistics route through the customer gate.

Surface water discharge from the attenuation pond was sampled on Monday and sent for laboratory analysis. Results are awaited, and the pond outlet valve remains closed until they are received. Silt fences along the access road have been inspected and two sections need repair after heavy rain. Ian Ashworth has asked the drainage team to clear the gully gratings before the next forecast rain.

## 7. Quality

Pressure gauges used on the nitrogen purge skid were sent for recalibration to an accredited laboratory. Certificates have now been returned and checked against the instrument register. Three gauges drifted outside tolerance and have been withdrawn, with replacements fitted and recorded on the calibration log held by Craig Brennan.

## 8. Logistics and interfaces

The tanker carrying nitrogen for the purge system will park on the concrete pad beside the compressor house. The driver must wear the correct PPE and remain in the vehicle cab during connection. The pad has an oil interceptor, so please keep the drain covers in place and report any spillage to the duty supervisor immediately.

## 9. Any other business

Permit to dig requests for the ground beside the existing flare stack are now being held until the utility survey is complete. Three requests were refused last week because the drawing did not match what was found on site. Craig Brennan will attend the next survey briefing and confirm which excavation areas can be released for digging.
